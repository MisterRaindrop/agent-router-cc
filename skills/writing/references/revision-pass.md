# Revising a draft

Order matters. Fixing sentences in a document with the wrong shape wastes the work.

## The order

**1. Structure.** Does the first paragraph carry the conclusion? Is there a summary paragraph to
delete? Are there four parallel cases in prose that want a table? Fix this first — it moves whole
paragraphs, and polished prose in the wrong place still has to go.

**2. Unexplained terms.** Pass 1 of `curse-of-knowledge.md`: underline every project-specific noun
phrase and check for an explanatory clause before it. Cheap, mechanical, high yield.

**3. Unsupported claims.** Every "safer", "cleaner", "better", "faster": either name the mechanism
or attach the number, or cut the claim. If it is a real judgement with no evidence, say that.

**4. Sentence level.** Now cut. One claim per sentence, no hedging, no padding, break the long
ones.

**5. Read it cold.** Ideally the next day; failing that, read it aloud. Padding is easier to hear
than to see.

## Telling padding from necessary repetition

Delete-and-reread is the test — but three kinds of repetition are load-bearing:

**A rule restated where it is applied.** A constraint stated once in a preamble and relied on
forty lines later should be restated at the point of use. The reader has forgotten.

**A number repeated next to a different conclusion.** The same measurement can support two claims.
Making the reader scroll back to check is worse than one repeated clause.

**A refusal condition stated in both the rule and the flow.** In command files especially. A model
following step 4 will not go back to the general principles.

Everything else that survives its own removal is padding. Common shapes:

- A sentence announcing what the next section will do.
- A closing sentence restating the opening one.
- "It is important to note that" — always deletable.
- A hedge around a claim you go on to rely on.
- A list of three examples where one carries the point.

## When you cut something load-bearing

You will. The tell is that a later paragraph stops making sense. Do not restore the sentence
verbatim: it was in the wrong place, which is why removing it looked safe. Put the fact where it
is needed.

## Delegating the pass

Under context pressure, hand steps 3–4 to a subagent — never step 1, which needs the whole
document in one head. Require a list of what was cut, and read it: a copy-editor that removed a
load-bearing qualifier is the failure mode, and the list is how you catch it.
